The module had helped to significantly improve my knowledge and understanding of secure software engineering principles and logic, particularly validation capabilities, defensive programming, and security-aware architectural design. Prior to the module, my focus was primarily on producing functional applications, whereas the later units highlighted the importance of thinking before designing and develop systems that solve problems and that are maintainable, testable, and resilient against misuse.
During Unit 7, I implemented in a very small prototype exercise, validation controls, password hashing and account protection mechanisms that followed some of the OWASP best practices, for instance, password handling using bcrypt which demonstrated the importance of plaintext credential storage prevention, while validation checks helped reduce the likelihood of weak, invalid, or malicious user input being executed and the inclusion of account lockout and rate-limiting concepts also underlined how secure credential workflows supports balance between usability and security requirements.
(OWASP Foundation, 2026).
Unit 10 had enabled me to improvement the practices of defensive programming within the Secure E-Learning Platform project, where validation logic was centralized within the business layer in order to provide consistency across modules and reduce duplication, such approach helps to improve maintainability while reducing the likelihood of invalid operations bypassing user interface controls. I have gained a very important learning result from these activities and that is, the recognition that secure coding is not necessarily limited to authentication alone, rather, security influences architectural decisions, validation strategies, testing practices, and maintainability throughout the software lifecycle.

(Units 1, 2, 6 and 10)
Those modules helped strengthen my knowledge of object-oriented programming principles including abstraction, encapsulation, inheritance, polymorphism, and modular decomposition. Unit 1 had presented inheritance hierarchies and abstract class structures through practical Python exercises involving Vehicle/Car and Shape/Animal examples.
(Lott, 2024).

Mainly comprehending the difference between interface enforcement and data hiding, however, enforcing abstract base classes helped to improve my experience and understanding about how abstraction supports consistency and extensibility across related objects. Unit 2 had helped to expand this understanding using the practical application of SOLID principles, refactoring the shopping system helped highlighting how tightly coupled logic can reduce maintainability and increase the difficulty of flexibility and future adaptation, applying the Dependency Inversion Principle (DIP) and Interface Segregation Principle (ISP) improved modularity and may result in reduced direct dependencies between components.

**1.3 Design Patterns and Maintainability**

(Units 3, 4, 5, 8, 9 and 11)
Using this module, I developed understanding of how design patterns can help to improve maintainability, extensibility, and separation of concerns when the designing of software systems.

**Strategy Pattern**

(Units 5, 8 and 9)
The Strategy Pattern was implemented through interchangeable payment and discount behaviors. Prior to refactoring, pricing and payment I implemented the Strategy Pattern through interchangeable payment and discount behaviors which, prior to refactoring, pricing and payment logic relied heavily on conditional statements, making the code difficult to extend and maintain. Separating these behaviors into strategy classes improved flexibility by allowing functionality to be extended without modifying existing business logic.
(Freeman and Robson, 2020, Chow, 2024).
Although the pattern introduced additional abstraction and more classes, the trade-off had become beneficial when additional pricing rules were implemented. This had helped to reinforce the importance of balancing simplicity with long-term maintainability.

**Factory Method**

(Units 3 and 9)
I explored Factory Method concepts through object creation scenarios where instantiation logic was delegated to specialized factory classes.
(Lott and Phillips, 2021). 
This technique had helped to improve scalability by reducing direct dependencies on concrete implementations and simplified future extension of additional providers and services.

During unit's 4 task, the Adapter, Bridge, and Composite patterns were presented in order be used in the development of a Collaborative Discussion initial post task and further collaborative discussion comments on other peer students structural design patterns work. 
(Freeman and Robson, 2020).
Using this implementation of patterns I managed to demonstrate how structural design methods supports integration flexibility, separation of concerns, and hierarchical object management. Although the implementation for the purpose of the collaborative discussion was relatively small in scale, it provided practical awareness of how architectural structure influences maintainability.

**Dependency Injection and IoC**

During unit 11 I implemented Dependency Injection and Inversion of Control (IoC). Using notification service abstractions, mock services during testing and SOLID principles, this had contributed to improved isolated unit testing and reduced coupling between components. A significant learning outcome was recognizing that design patterns should not be applied unnecessarily, some patterns improved extensibility considerably, while others added complexity that would not be justified within smaller applications.
(Marcotte and Zebdi, 2022).
